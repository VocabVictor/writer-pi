Ferris Supply sits on the corner of Ninth and Alder, five minutes from my apartment. It has been in business since 1961. I started going there three years ago, when a cabinet hinge gave out, and I have kept going back for screws, keys, and whatever piece of a plumbing problem I have not identified yet.

Ruth Ferris runs the counter herself. She is the founder's granddaughter. The store has been in the family the whole time. Most days she has the place to herself except for Mortar, the shop cat, who sleeps on the seed rack and has never caught anything.

Screws are sold out of wooden drawers, not packages. Each drawer has a hand-inked label. You tell Ruth the size and she pulls the right one without looking up. There are more drawers than I have ever counted. In the back room they still cut keys by hand and thread pipe, and you can hear the work from the counter.

Ruth keeps a ledger of house accounts for a dozen longtime customers. No deposits, no receipts. If you are on the list you pay at the end of the month, and that is the whole arrangement. I am not on the list. I have watched her write in it.

The store closes at 5:30, sharp. I have been inside when it happens: Ruth flips the sign to CLOSED while still talking to whoever is there, and the conversation keeps going. Nobody gets hurried out.

On Saturdays her nephew works the register, and every price lookup comes out wrong. Ruth calls the right number from wherever she is. The rest of the week the prices come out right the first time, because Ruth is the one doing the lookups.
